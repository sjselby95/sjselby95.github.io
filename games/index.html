Games.

Small games I built for my kids. They run entirely in the browser — no accounts, no ads, nothing sent anywhere. Scores are kept on your own device.

For the six-year-old

Ages 4+ Bone Quarry Brush dirt off a buried skeleton, rebuild it bone by bone, then hear it read aloud. Ten dig sites — and four of them are not dinosaurs.
Reflexes Plate Run An endless runner where the plates on a Stegosaurus's back are the health bar. Tap to jump, hold the corner to duck, and it never stops speeding up.
Timing Thagomizer Predators close in from both sides. Swing the spiked tail left, stomp right — but only when they're inside the ring. Swinging at nothing leaves you open.

For the littlest one

No reading, no score, nothing to lose, and no way to get stuck. Just touch the screen and something happens.

Ages 2+ Tide Pool Tap the water and an Alaska sea animal appears. Touch one and it says its own name out loud — otter, puffin, seal, octopus, jellyfish, sea lion, sea star, crab.
Loud Monster Music Six monsters, six noises — power chords, drums, and a growl. They blink, they breathe, and they yell when you poke them. Headphones optional, patience required.
Calm Snow Palace Tap to make it snow, drag to draw frost, and it piles up on the ground. Tap the snow queen and her dress changes color. The northern lights run the whole time.
Quiet Finger Paint Drag a finger and light trails out behind it, playing a note as it goes. Nothing is ever wrong, and it slowly fades itself clean so the screen never fills up.

Built with a kid in the room. Bone Quarry and Tide Pool both speak out loud with baked-in narration, so they double as vocabulary practice. Every page here is a single static file — no build step, no framework, no third-party scripts beyond the web font.
